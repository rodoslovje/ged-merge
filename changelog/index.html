Slovenščina

Changelog

New features and major improvements in GED Merge, newest first.

2026-08-13

  • An event another program named in its own code now reads by name — a MyHeritage file's unmarried couple shows as “Partners (MyHeritage)” and its marriages as “Marriage (MARR)”, with the original code in the tooltip and in the field you edit.

2026-08-12

  • Bringing in a branch now adds its people as records of their own — it joins onto a person you already have only where the two records could be one person, and the save preview names every join it made on its own judgement rather than yours.
  • The addresses list now finds one house written two ways and offers to join them — a note of yours behind the number, one of the two street names a renamed street left, or the street spelt short, each naming what it would become.
  • The naming check now lists every finding and lets you write an address yourself — the ✎ beside a house rewrites it on every event standing there, and no finding waits behind the search box any more.

2026-08-11

  • The Timeline now reaches as many generations as the Generations setting says, in both directions at once — grandparents open the chart and grandchildren close it, each named by their exact kinship, with every couple standing together and a later marriage entering at its wedding.
  • The Fan and Circle now draw eight rings and count whoever stands above them — the outermost ring carries a “+N” marker that re-draws the chart from that ancestor.
  • Charts now write names the way the Name-display settings do, and gain a Married-surname toggle of their own — a pedigree can print maiden names alone while the rest of the app goes on showing both.
  • Hiding living people now hides their marriages — a couple with one living partner shows no wedding year or place on any diagram.
  • H draws the chart for your start person — on every kind: the pedigrees, the Timeline, the relationship diagram, the map and the report.
  • People imported from a matches CSV now arrive with a sex, read from their given name — the index export has no sex column at all, so Marija now comes in a woman and Jurij a man, while a name that could be either is left empty rather than guessed.
  • The compare panel now marks a disagreement over the name or the birth year in red — the fields that decide whether two records are the same person at all, in the same red the compare tree gives that person's node.

2026-08-09

  • A file that names no country anywhere is now placed by the directories you have loaded — a file that writes Kranj and Poljane thousands of times and Slovenia never is recognised as Slovenian because Slovenia's directory is the one holding its places, and Settings says whether the answer came from the file or from the directories.
  • The naming check's answers now name the municipality and the country — six same-named villages read as six different places instead of six identical lines, and picking one writes it out in full, in your file's own layout, unless your value says something a register cannot compose.
  • The naming check's rename box now takes an address and asks the registers — correct the settlement, move the house onto the event's own ADDR line, and complete a name your file has never written from the directories, coordinate and all, in one step.
  • The naming check now moves a house's events into the settlement the register files it under — the proposal is taken on the finding itself, where you read it, instead of being repeated by hand on the Addresses tab.
  • Place directories can now be downloaded without allowing online lookups — fetching a country's published dataset asks nothing about your file, so GURS, DGU and OpenStreetMap are always offered.
  • The map now names the house an event stands at — its address sits beside the place in the marker list and in the tooltip, so nine events on one village's coordinate read as the houses they are.

2026-08-08

  • A place that names no country is now taken to be in your file's own country — read off the file (the country most of its places name, shown and changeable in Settings → GEDCOM), it groups those places under it, searches that country's directories alone, lets the compliance check judge them at last, and will write the country into them for you.
  • The country buttons above the place and address lists now name countries — one flagged button per country in your own language however your file spells it, a place written down to Ohio or Tasmania counted under the country it belongs to, and a patron saint or a stray date counted as no country at all.
  • An address recording one house under two names is now checked as the two addresses it is — Labore 4 / Škofjeloška 4 takes the register's spelling for the half it still knows and keeps the historical name beside it.

2026-08-07

  • Your places can now be checked against the place directories you have loaded — every place in a country any of them covers is held against it, and what disagrees is listed with the register's own wording to take in its place: a name it does not know, one it spells differently, a settlement it files under another jurisdiction, a house address that has ended up inside the place, and a coordinate nowhere near where it puts the place.
  • Addresses can now be held against the register too — compliance is a page of its own beside Geocoding, and opening its addresses tab names the streets the register spells differently and the houses it files under another village, puts each on a map, and can rewrite them all to the register's spelling in one step.
  • A place written with its county is now read as one — the compliance check holds each place's parent against the register at the level your own places write, offers its correction in that same level and your own wording of it, and shows what your file's FORM labels call each part of a place.
  • A place naming a county rather than a village is now recognised as one — no settlement is hunted for it, and where the county stands written twice over or a level has been left blank, the row offers the same place one comma shorter.
  • Slovenian and Croatian addresses can now be placed from a register kept in your browser — download a country's house numbers once, and the addresses list looks them up by itself as it opens, each on its own building, with no network and nothing to press.
  • A house no register will ever answer can now be set aside — old numbering or a farm long gone leaves the addresses worklist and stops counting as work left, and comes back whenever you ask for it.
  • Abbreviated village names now match their spelt-out form — Sp. Idrija finds the register's Spodnja Idrija, and the same understanding of Sv., Zg., Sr., Vel., Mal., Dol. and Gor. runs through the compliance check, the lookups and the wider search.
  • Look wider now reaches a place you have misspelt — names that merely resemble the one written, which no search by spelling can find: Mrkopolje turns up the register's Mrkopalj.
  • A place is now staged for writing by the coordinate you pick — the tick box is gone from the places list, an opened row arrives with its map, and every geocoding list says what its last write did in the same words, in the same place.

2026-08-06

  • Matched parents now decide which incoming person a child really is — the counterpart inside the matched family wins over a same-named stranger carrying no dates or places, and nobody is paired with a person who married after they had died.
  • A marriage to someone else merges as a family of its own. The review sets an incoming union whose partner is plainly a different person beside the existing marriage instead of over it, so confirming adds that partner and their children as a second family and leaves the first untouched.
  • A branch import now stops where the two files disagree about a parent — the incoming parent and the ancestors above them stay out instead of landing in your file attached to nothing, and the save preview names who was kept and what stayed behind.
  • The first letters of each name find the person. Every box you type a name into — the relative pickers, search, the start person, find-in-chart, the match list — matches each word on its own, in any order and with the accents optional, so sebas kala finds Sebastjan Kalan.
  • Your browser's Back button walks back through the people you opened in Edit, one at a time, and asks about leaving the page only once there is nothing left to go back to.
  • The health check catches a child born after a parent had died — a wrong family link, or a date that belongs to somebody else, often a same-named infant.
  • The media-folder prompt answers three ways — connect now, later, or never again — and the browser's “upload” notice takes a Don't warn me again tick, both restorable in Settings.
  • A tree you started from nothing names itself — the first person you add becomes the start person, and both the download and the open file take their surname instead of new-tree.
  • A place's records are now a click on its count — opening a place shows the places under it, the ✎ that renames it stands beside its name, and the rename completes only from the names beside it, never another town's houses.

2026-08-05

  • Keyboard entry, end to end. Enter runs from the name through the birth date to its place, and ⌥⇧ adds events, notes, sources, media, names and relatives without leaving the field you are typing in.
  • Quick-add event buttons move under the event list, on ⌥⇧1–9; an event a person can have only one of leads to the row that already holds it.
  • Naming both parents joins the family they already have — the child lands with their other children, not in a second record for the same couple.
  • A new wife can take the husband's surname as her married name — optional, in Settings.
  • Person lookups read like the person cards — sex colour, lifespan, record id — and find someone by any name they hold.
  • Dropdown menus match the app, with grouped headings, a ✓ on the current choice and keyboard navigation.
  • The place finder offers the place itself, above its houses, and brings the place's own coordinate.
  • The Grid chart packs descendants tighter, one trunk branching from the couple to all their children.

2026-08-04

  • Place a whole village at one coordinate. Tick the addresses no register can answer — old numbering, a farm long gone — pick one position, and all of them take it in a single undoable step. Type a prefix to tick just the houses that share it.
  • Every address is reviewed, not just the numbered ones. A hamlet with no house number, a house known by its farm name, an address abroad — all listed, marked by hand only, and placeable like the rest.
  • OpenStreetMap places the addresses the register cannot. A hamlet, a house with no number, an address abroad — search OSM from the row; hits sharing a name are told apart by what they are and by numbered pins on one map.
  • Place lookups answer in the file's own language. A file writing United States is answered in English, one writing Slovenija in Slovenian, whatever the interface language — so a register name matches the file it joins.
  • Every address shows the position it holds — its own, or the settlement's it inherits. The address opens one panel with a map to pick on, the register lookup and manual entry; houses found there are listed on the row.
  • The geocoding lists keep only the unplaced. A place or house that carries its position leaves its worklist; tick Show already placed to bring the finished ones back — to check a position, or pick a better one that replaces it.
  • A renamed street is looked up under both names. An address recorded as Labore 4 / Škofjeloška 4 is read as two whole addresses, and the register is asked about each.
  • A pin marks every position. Coordinates now carry the map pin wherever they are printed — in the geocoding lists, dropdowns and map tooltips — so the numbers read as a place on the ground, muted until something is placed there and green once it is.
  • Croatian places from the official register. Settings › Map downloads 24 000 Croatian settlements, villages and hamlets from the register of geographical names, each naming its county — recognised in English too — and carrying its Italian, Hungarian and historical names.

2026-08-03

  • Geocoding organized by country. Buttons above the geocoding list show each country's share and narrow the list to one, others split it by the work left — confident, possible, ambiguous, no proposal, decided — and a row's count opens its people.
  • Geocoding knows parent-qualified names. A place written with its municipality now finds the register's longer name — Vinji vrh under Semič finds Vinji Vrh pri Semiču — and every proposal names its municipality.
  • Take the official name. A row matched to the register's longer name — Cerovec under Semič to Cerovec pri Črešnjevcu — adopts it with one click, and a button renames every confident row shown at once, coordinates included.
  • Places and addresses switch as tabs. The geocoding page's two lists sit behind tabs with row counts, so the addresses no longer wait below a thousand places; the search box narrows whichever is open.
  • Address list filters by lookup state. Chips split the house addresses into not yet looked up, with results, no result and picked, counting what each click shows, and an address's person count opens the people behind it.
  • Select confident for addresses. One click stages every address the register answered with exactly one house; a hit the register files under another settlement stays for review.
  • Rename an address. ✎ on an address row rewrites it on every event at that house — ADDR line or packed place value alike — and completes from the other addresses at that place, so duplicate spellings merge into one row.
  • Foreign places show their region. Downloaded place directories now carry each place's county or state — Croatian županija, Austrian Bundesland — shown beside the candidate, in your file's own spelling when it names the division, and telling same-named places apart.
  • Choose the comma form in place names. Settings › GEDCOM now decides between Kranj, Slovenija and Kranj,Slovenija, and Normalize & batch writes your choice through the whole file.
  • New places say what their levels are. A place taken from a register, or picked from one your file already has, now carries the standard FORM line naming each level — in your file's own wording per country, Place,Upravna Enota,Country or Place,Bundesland,Country.
  • Synchronized map view. The Overlays picker can split the map into halves that move as one, each drawing its own pick of the layers, with the same event markers on both. A PNG export saves the pair side by side.
  • Add every free map layer in one go. The preset menu in Settings ends with an entry that adds all the layers you do not yet have, and the map's Overlays list scrolls once it grows long.

2026-08-02

  • A scale bar on the map. Bottom left, it says what a length on screen is on the ground — the question the cadastral overlays raise constantly, since zoom level alone does not answer it.
  • Franciscean cadastre at 1:2880. Thirty-two municipalities from the Archives of the Republic of Slovenia: an unbroken run from Škofja Loka through Kranj to Šenčur and Naklo, seven in Bela krajina, two in Dolenjska, and Ljubljana with three suburbs.
  • Place directories move to Settings › Map. Add them there from GURS, from any country you pick out of OpenStreetMap, or from a GeoNames file, and see what is loaded. Geocode places links to them.
  • Large countries download by region. Where OpenStreetMap cannot send a whole country at once — the United States, say — GED Merge lists its regions and fetches the one you pick, or all of them in turn, into the same directory.
  • Matches CSV: the relatives in a row are matches too. Partners, parents and children named inside a row are now offered as candidates of their own, and a person the CSV names several times arrives as one connected record.
  • Batch filters for missing names, relatives and unions. Filter people by a name part the record lacks, by whether they have a partner, children or parents, and by what their unions record — no marriage, or no event at all.
  • Batch action: married names from the partners. Every union contributes its partner's surname, in marriage order, written the way the file already writes them; people whose union records no marriage arrive unchecked, so you decide.
  • Enter finishes an edit field. It saves what you typed and gives the keyboard back, so E, M or T switches mode straight after editing (notes keep Enter for line breaks).

2026-08-01

  • Batch actions on filtered people. In Tools, stack filters (image, age, kinship, missing event …), then attach media, cite a source, mark people deceased or convert event types across every match in one undoable step; saved filters show live counts.
  • Undated people can take an estimated birth year. A new batch action writes an ABT birth date estimated from dated parents, siblings, spouse or children, previewing each person's year in the match list; re-running reaches further, through estimates already written.
  • The health check finds events outside a person's lifespan. An occupation dated before the birth, or a residence or marriage after the death, is now listed on the person; burial, cremation and probate may follow a death.
  • The health check flags the living older than 99. A person with no death record but born over 99 years ago is listed — almost always a missing death, and the batch mark-as-deceased action closes them.
  • Add and edit sources in Tools → Sources. Add source files a record cited by no one yet, with the repository autodetected and pickable; ✎ on a source or repository row edits it in place.
  • Source media show as picture trays. With the media folder loaded, a source's pages, cutouts and unattached media become thumbnails with usage counts; the viewer opens with editable details and the referencing people beside it.
  • Create a repository right in the source dialog. The Repository dropdown's New repository… takes a name and files the source under it — when adding a source, or moving one with ✎.
  • A person's families follow marriage order. Edit, charts, the timeline and the reports now list unions by marriage date; an undated marriage takes its place from its children's births.
  • Every married surname is shown. A person married more than once now carries all married surnames, comma-separated in the same marriage order, wherever Show married surname is on.
  • Your edits now outrank a confirmed match. A field you change after confirming keeps your value instead of being overwritten on save, and the preview lists each field it left alone under Kept as in your file.
  • FamilySearch IDs show on the person in Edit. The chip links to the person's FamilySearch page and is edited in place; the Merge comparison now shows and carries the ids too.
  • A parent family that names no parent can be removed. Its own − unlinks the person from the leftover family, and the health check lists these alongside family records nobody is in.
  • The landing page now shows the whole workbench. Four mode cards — Edit, Merge, Charts & maps, Tools — replace the feature list, with the app's key figures and its newest changes beside them.

2026-07-31

  • Address geocoding reads the house out of the place. Files that write no address line — Črni vrh 35, or a house packed into the place value — now get the same grouped register lookup, filed under their settlement.
  • Place an address by hand. Every address row now carries the map pin: type a coordinate, pick a point off the map, or search OpenStreetMap for a house the register does not know.
  • Houses sit under their settlement. A place value carrying its house number (Krupa 7) is now filed under Krupa in the place explorer, instead of standing beside it.
  • The Spezialkarte overlay now covers the whole monarchy. All 805 sheets of the Habsburg 1:75 000 survey are in, Bohemia to Transylvania, each placed on modern coordinates rather than the survey's own.
  • See the base map before you choose it. Settings → Map now draws a sample of the picked background, with any layer marked Default over it. Ticking a layer takes the sample to the ground it covers, at a zoom where it is drawn.
  • Find a person in the Report or on the Map. Both now carry the charts' find box: the Report jumps to the numbered entry, the Map flies to the event and opens it. On the Map a place name works too.
  • Ctrl/Cmd+F opens the search box of whatever you are looking at — the match list, a Tools list, a chart or the Map. Where a view has none, the browser's own find takes over as before.
  • The app fits a phone. The top bar is now a single row — the mode tabs and a ☰ menu carrying the loaded files, the start person and the header buttons — and a full-page diagram or map takes the whole screen.
  • Charts zoom with a pinch. On a touch screen, two fingers zoom any diagram toward the pinch point; one finger scrolls as before.

2026-07-30

  • The health check finds detached groups. Up to nine people linked only to each other and to nobody else in the file are now listed once, on their youngest member — the place to start looking for where the group belongs.
  • The health check finds people with two sets of parents. A person who is a birth child in two families at once is now listed, naming both couples. Adoptive and foster links are left alone.
  • The save preview says when your file's encoding changes. A file read as ANSEL, ANSI or Windows-1250 is saved as UTF-8, and the health check and preview now say so, so you know to check that it still imports elsewhere.
  • Remove dangling references in one click. A new fix drops pointers whose target record is missing — source, note and media citations, associations, and family links inside an event. Undoable, like every other fix.
  • Repeated ancestors are marked on the Fan and the Circle. Where cousins married, the shared ancestors fill two wedges; the second now carries a → badge on its outer edge that takes you to the one drawing their line.
  • Back returns to the chart you came from. Re-rooting a chart — Set as root, a +7 pill, an off-chart search hit — is now a step of its own, so Back retraces the people you rooted on.
  • Choose the map background. Settings → Map now offers five backgrounds that need no account: CARTO, OpenStreetMap, terrain, and Esri satellite and topographic. Terrain shows the valley a family stayed in, satellite whether the house still stands.
  • Places show their coordinates. The place explorer prints the coordinate the file holds for each place; click it for a mini map. A place written at two different spots is flagged with the count, and both are pinned.

2026-07-29

  • Every chart now prints on paper you choose. Printing any chart — radial and timeline ones included — now starts by asking which paper you are printing on, and fits the diagram to it.
  • Print a chart on big paper. The paper list now reaches A2, A1 and A0, and Custom takes any size in millimetres, so a plotter roll prints as one enormous sheet.
  • The print now fills the paper. Every sheet is now scaled to fill the page, and the new One sheet size shrinks a whole diagram onto the single sheet you chose, uncut.
  • Sharp text in exported charts. Names in an exported chart stay real text instead of a picture, so a PDF stays sharp however far you zoom and prints in seconds.
  • Choose which parents a merged duplicate keeps. The duplicate comparison now carries one choice on the Parents block — keep the left record's parents, take the candidate's, or keep both — instead of always bringing both parent families across.
  • Merge a duplicated branch in the right order. Relatives that are a separate record on each side now come with a match score and a tick box: ticked ones are merged first, so their duplicate families collapse into one.
  • Merge duplicated children too. Children that are a separate record on each side now appear with a match score and a tick box, and ticked ones are merged after the pair, once they share one set of parents.

2026-07-28

  • Choose how many generations a chart shows. A descendants chart drew every generation it could find, and in a large tree each further generation multiplies what is on screen. The chart settings (⚙) now carry Generations: step down from All and the Tree, Grid, Fan, Circle, the Report and the places Map all stop that many generations from the root person — the reading is 4 of 9, so you always see how much is left out. Where a chart is cut, the last person drawn carries a +7 badge counting the descendants (or ancestors) not shown; click it and the chart continues from that person. Page headers and every export name the generations drawn, and a shortened report closes with a line about the ones it left off.
  • Print a wide chart across several sheets. A family diagram that pans comfortably on screen rarely survives being squeezed onto one page — fitted to A4, the names stop being readable. The Tree and the Grid now offer PDF in sheets in the Export menu: choose the paper (A4, A3 or Letter), the orientation and the print size, see how many sheets the diagram will take, and print the set — an ancestors chart doubles in width every generation, so the print size is what really decides whether that is five sheets or fifty. It is cut at its branches, never mid-box — a branch that doesn't fit carries on over a sheet of its own, and the person it was cut from keeps a numbered circle 3 pointing at the sheet that picks the line up. Every sheet is headed with its number and where it continues from, and all of them print at the same scale, so the printed set tapes together into one chart.
  • Find a person in the open chart. A search box on every chart page moves the view to the person you type and flashes them, with a counter and ‹ › for the several places a shared ancestor occupies — where the file-wide search would have redrawn the diagram around them instead. If the name isn't on this chart but is in your file, the box offers that person in one click.
  • Nobody is drawn or counted twice. Where two descendants of the same person married each other, the couple's children were drawn under both parents and the descendant count grew accordingly. A branch is now drawn at its first position only; a later position keeps the person and their marriage but stays empty and carries a → badge you can click to go to the position that does — and the Ancestors / Descendants counts are counts of people.
  • Add a person attached to nobody. Until now a new person could only be born into the tree as someone's father, mother, partner or child. A ＋ button in the header — or the N key — now creates one linked to no one, which is what the start of a new branch needs; and a search that finds nobody offers to add the name you just typed. The start page gains Start a new tree as well, beside the sample trees, so a tree that exists nowhere yet can begin with its first person instead of an import.
  • Fix a broken link where you find it. Every broken-link finding in the Health check now carries its own Remove link button, so a single dead pointer can go without repairing the whole file — a link to a family that no longer exists is invisible in Edit, since the missing family draws nothing there.

2026-07-27

  • Add a place your file has never used, from the registers. The place field on an event used to know only the places already in your file. Type one it has never seen and the list now ends with Look this place up in the registers — the imported gazetteer, and, on the online opt-in, Slovenia's address register (GURS), GOV and OpenStreetMap. Each offer arrives written the way your file writes places (same layout, separator, country spelling and number of jurisdiction levels), and picking one fills in the place, the house address when you typed a number the register recognised, and its coordinate — in one undoable step.
  • The address field looks houses up too. Type 21 or Hafnarjeva pot 21 in an event's address and the same row searches GURS and OpenStreetMap for that house within the event's place. An offer reads place · address and carries the settlement the register really files the house under — so where a street belongs to the town that absorbed your village, taking it corrects the place along with the address and the coordinate. Needs online lookups; house numbers are not in the offline gazetteer.
  • Split a place that hides a settlement of its own. When a file records a whole hamlet under its neighbour — every Klošter 12 filed under Gradac, Metlika, Slovenia, usually because that is the post office — each place group in Geocode places › Addresses now offers Move to another place…: tick the addresses that belong elsewhere, name the destination, and only those events move. Renaming the whole place, which would move the events that really do belong there too, is no longer the only option.
  • The geocoding tools complete a place from the registers too. The rename box in Geocode places and the destination in Move to another place… are now the same place field as in Edit, so a place your file has never written can be looked up in the gazetteer, GURS, GOV or OpenStreetMap and taken whole — chain, address and coordinate. A renamed value is placed in the same step, and moved addresses land on the destination's own coordinate (the register's, or the one your file already records for it) instead of losing the coordinate they had.
  • GURS says which settlement an address is really in. A house number the address register cannot find under the settlement your file names is now looked up as a settlement in its own right. That finds the house — and where the register files it elsewhere, the group reports it (6 addresses are in Klošter (Metlika), not Gradac) and offers the move already filled in. Slovenian places only, on the same online opt-in as the other register searches.
  • Same name, different municipality. The Slovenian gazetteer now carries each settlement's občina, so the two Soteska are told apart — Soteska (Kamnik) and Soteska (Dolenjske Toplice) — instead of both matching 100 % with nothing to choose between them. Where your place names the municipality too, the right one is picked automatically. GOV results name their parent place the same way. Re-download the Slovenian gazetteer once to get the občine.
  • One shape for the whole geocoding page. The place, address and conflict lists now open the same kind of row, draw their map only when asked, and collect picks until the list's own button at the top writes them. Candidates from every source read alike too — Stopiče (Novo mesto), Stožice (Ljubljana) — instead of one printing its whole address chain.
  • Settle contradictory coordinates on a map. Where one place and address carries two coordinates, Geocode places expands the row to a map whose numbered circles count their events — the outlier is obvious. Pick the right one (or click the map) and every event is rewritten to it.
  • One coordinate for every event at the same address. Placing an event whose place and address other events share now offers — ticked by default — to write that coordinate onto all of them, so a house is pinned once rather than event by event.
  • Default map overlays. A layer marked Default in Settings → Map is switched on wherever a map is drawn: every Places map opens with it already on, and the small per-person and geocoding maps show it too — so a favourite map (say the cadastre or a historical survey) no longer has to be ticked again each time.
  • Map layers stack in the order you choose. The layer list is now the stacking order — first on top — with ▲▼ buttons in Settings to rearrange it. House numbers or parcel boundaries no longer vanish under a historical map switched on at the same time.
  • Click the small maps for layer details. The per-person and geocoding maps now answer a click the way the full map does — with a default house-number layer on, clicking a building gives its official address without leaving the person you are editing.
  • Fit the map to what is plotted. A third button under the zoom controls frames everything currently drawn — on the Places map and the small per-person and geocoding maps alike. Marker tooltips now name the place too, with the street address for a single event.
  • The phone layout gives the screen back to your data. On a phone the frame around the content had grown larger than the content itself. The six Tools sub-tools, which filled the whole first screen as stacked cards, are now one row of chips you swipe sideways — a health check shows its first findings straight away. Chart and map pages keep a long name to two lines instead of four, scroll the chart-kind switcher rather than clipping it, and drop the page footer, so a map now gets a good deal more of the screen. Elsewhere the footer no longer sits pinned over your list — the page scrolls as one, with the header staying put and the footer arriving at the end of the content. Paddings across the app step down, and a long name no longer pushes the editor's fields off the right edge.
  • The match counter follows the filter. In Merge, the ‹ › arrows beside a match step through the filtered list, but the counter beside them was reading the unfiltered one — so a filter that hid everything still showed "1 of 34" next to two arrows that did nothing. The counter and the arrows now describe the same list.
  • Copy an event to other records. An event's type dropdown now offers Copy event to…, a checkbox list of the records it can go to — people for a personal event, families for a family one, with the person's own household first. The whole event travels (date, place with its coordinate, address, notes, sources), so a newly recorded residence reaches the rest of the family in one undoable step.

2026-07-26

  • Central Europe in 1797, before Napoleon. A new overlay preset draws Schraembl's Neueste Generalkarte von Deutschland (Vienna, 1797): the German lands, the Alps and the northern Adriatic in the Holy Roman Empire's last years, with the territories, borders and German place spellings of the time. It reaches back a generation further than any overlay so far, which began in 1820.
  • Slovenia's detailed topographic map, down to 1:2500. The GURS Topographic map overlay is now the colour Osnovna karta that the national land-survey viewer itself draws — one layer that stays legible from the whole country down to 1:2500, where individual buildings, contours, field boundaries, paths and toponyms appear. It replaces the previous 1:50 000 preset.
  • The older 1:5000 survey plan too. A separate preset adds the black-and-white Temeljni topografski načrt 1:5000, which records the buildings, field patterns and paths of its own, earlier survey — useful where the modern map no longer shows what your ancestor knew.

2026-07-25

  • Health check spots children by two fathers at once. A new check flags a mother whose children with one partner are interleaved in time with her children by another — impossible in life, and usually a child or a whole sibling set attached to the wrong family. Consecutive marriages and adopted children are left alone.
  • House-precise coordinates from the address register. Where a place or an event's address names a house number (Šentvid pri Stični 23), Slovenia's address register (GURS) now pins the actual building instead of the settlement's centre. A pin beside the place in Edit opens a small map to review the coordinate.
  • Health check tells addresses apart. Coordinates are now judged per place and address, so two addresses in one settlement may legitimately differ — while the same place and address carrying two different coordinates is reported as the contradiction it is.
  • Slovenian place coordinates in one click. Tools → Geocode places can now fetch all 6035 Slovenian settlements straight from the national land-survey register (GURS), instead of hunting down a gazetteer file. Bilingual Italian and Hungarian names come along, so Isola finds Izola. Stored in your browser, so it keeps working offline.
  • Duplicate finder handles big files. The list now starts at a high match score (≥ 85) so it stays short and actionable, with a score picker to reveal weaker matches on demand. Records that all look like the same person are grouped into clusters — a same-name “parallel branch” folds into one row you can review pair by pair or Reject all at once. Each pair also shows both records' GEDCOM ids, so same-name look-alikes are easy to tell apart.
  • WMS map overlays. The Places map can now draw OGC WMS layers over the base map, not just tile templates — with one-click presets for Slovenia's national GURS maps: historical and current orthophoto, topographic map, cadastral parcels and cadastral municipalities, house numbers, settlements and municipalities. Several layers answer a click on the map: house numbers give the full address (street, number, settlement, post code), a parcel its number, cadastral municipality and area, and the cadastral-municipality layer the katastrska občina with its number.
