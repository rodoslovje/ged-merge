Privacy Policy

What stays on your device, what can leave it, and who processes what. Written to be read — if anything here is unclear, ask.

Effective date: 31 July 2026 · Last updated: 31 July 2026 · Operator: Luka Renko, Slovenia · support@gedmerge.com

In short

GED Merge runs entirely in your web browser. Your GEDCOM and CSV files are never uploaded — they are read, matched, merged, charted and saved on your own device, and we have no access to them. Two features, both off by default, make network requests once you switch them on: online lookups and base-map tiles. Neither ever sends your file; what goes out is a single value you asked to look up, or the map area you are looking at. Section 2 says exactly what goes where.

1Your file

Any GEDCOM (.ged) or CSV file you open is read by JavaScript inside your browser. Parsing, normalization, matching, merging, editing, the charts, the reports and every maintenance tool run on your own computer — most of it in a background thread of the same browser tab. There is no upload endpoint and no API: the server serves the application's own files and nothing else.

Saving produces an ordinary download to your device. Media — photos and documents — are read from a folder you point at, on your device only; they are never copied anywhere.

There is no registration, no account, no email address, no payment and no analytics of any kind. The application loads no third-party scripts and no third-party fonts (the IBM Plex fonts it uses are served from this site). Once loaded, it keeps working with no network connection at all.

2Optional online services

Two settings, both off by default, are the only parts of GED Merge that contact anyone else. Nothing is sent while you type — a request happens when you press a search button, accept a lookup, or open a map with tiles enabled.

Worth knowing

A place name, a house number or an archive URL is genealogical information even though it is not a person: a parish-register link says which family you are researching. That is why these lookups are opt-in. The four CORS relays are operated by third parties outside the European Economic Area, and we have no data-processing agreement or transfer safeguard with any of them — if a particular URL is sensitive to you, leave the setting off and type the title by hand.

2.2 Base-map tiles — Settings › Map

Every map — the Places map, the small maps under a person's events, the coordinate picker — draws on square image tiles fetched from a provider. The request reveals the area and zoom level you are looking at, which for a family tree usually means the region your ancestors lived in. It never carries your file, your pins or your names.

  • Base maps: CARTO, OpenStreetMap, OpenTopoMap and Esri (ArcGIS Online), or any tile URL you enter yourself.
  • Historical and register overlays, when you switch one on: Slovenia's GURS services (ipi.eprostor.gov.si), swisstopo (wmts.geo.admin.ch), IGN France (data.geopf.fr), and tiles.gedmerge.com, which is our own server (see §4). Clicking a GURS overlay to identify a feature sends that one map coordinate to GURS.

Leave the setting off and every map draws on a world outline bundled with the application, with no request at all.

2.3 The status of these providers

These services are independent controllers, not our processors: they decide for themselves what they log and for how long, under their own privacy policies. We have no agreement with them on your behalf and receive nothing back from them about you. Your legal basis for these transfers is your consent, given by switching the setting on and withdrawable at any time by switching it off — see §6. Accepted place lookups are remembered in your browser (§3) so the same place is not asked about twice.

3Data stored in your browser

GED Merge stores things on your own device so it can remember your preferences and, if you ask it to, your work. All of it stays there; none of it is readable by us.

Removing it: Clear locally stored data in Settings › Data deletes all of the above except your preferences — the cached files and progress, the remembered place lookups and imported gazetteers, and the media-folder handle. Clearing this site's data in your browser removes the preferences as well.

4Server logs

Delivering the application itself is a normal web request, and it passes through two providers: Hetzner Online GmbH / Hetzner Cloud (origin hosting, Germany) and Cloudflare, Inc. (CDN and security proxy, USA). Both may log standard technical data — IP address, browser type, requested file, timestamp — for delivery, maintenance and abuse protection. These logs contain no genealogical data and no file content, because none is ever sent.

tiles.gedmerge.com, which serves the historical map overlays we host ourselves, runs on the same infrastructure. A request to it records which map tile was fetched, and therefore which region you were looking at, alongside the usual technical fields. It sets no cookies, and it is only contacted if you have enabled map tiles and switched on one of those overlays.

We do not combine these logs with anything else, and we build no profiles from them.

5Cookies

GED Merge sets no cookies of its own and uses no tracking cookies, advertising cookies or third-party analytics. Cloudflare, as our security proxy, may set strictly necessary cookies such as __cf_bm or cf_clearance to tell browsers from bots. These are infrastructure cookies: they do not identify you personally and do not follow you to other sites.

The local storage and IndexedDB entries listed in §3 are either functional (your own preferences) or switched on by you. None of them is used for tracking or shared with anyone, which is why the site shows no consent banner. If your jurisdiction treats them otherwise, clearing this site's data removes them.

6Legal basis (GDPR)

Controller: Luka Renko, Slovenia (European Union), support@gedmerge.com. A postal address is available on request. The scale of processing does not require a Data Protection Officer.

  • Your genealogical data — you are the controller, not us. Your file never reaches us, so we are not in a position to control, disclose or delete it. If it contains living people, the responsibility for handling their data is yours; see the Terms § 3 and the redaction tools described in the User's Guide § 18.
  • Server logs — legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR) in delivering, maintaining and securing the site.
  • Optional online services — consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). The two settings in §2 are the consent; switching a setting off withdraws it for everything that follows.
  • Processors: Hetzner (Germany, within the EU, under Art. 28 GDPR) and Cloudflare (USA). Cloudflare's processing may involve a transfer to the United States; it currently participates in the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and offers Standard Contractual Clauses and a Data Processing Addendum, which we rely on as the safeguard.
  • The lookup and tile providers in §2 are not our processors. They are independent controllers, several of them outside the EEA and reached without a transfer instrument on our side. That is precisely why those features are opt-in and default to off.

We carry out no automated decision-making and no profiling, we run no advertising, and we have never sold or shared data for anyone else's purposes — there is nothing to sell.

7Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right of access (Art. 15), rectification (Art. 16), erasure (Art. 17), restriction (Art. 18), data portability (Art. 20), objection to processing based on legitimate interests (Art. 21), and withdrawal of consent at any time (Art. 7(3)).

In practice these are directed at the technical logs described in §4, since that is all that exists on our side: your genealogical data is never in our hands, so there is nothing there for us to produce, correct or erase — the architecture preserves those rights rather than the paperwork. Consent for the optional online services is withdrawn by switching the setting off; data already stored in your browser is removed as described in §3.

Write to support@gedmerge.com to exercise any of these. You also have the right to lodge a complaint with a supervisory authority — in Slovenia, the Information Commissioner (Informacijski pooblaščenec, ip-rs.si) — or with the authority where you live or work.

8Retention and security

We keep no database of users, because there are none. Server logs are retained only for the short periods our providers apply for security and diagnostics, under their own retention policies; we do not export or archive them. Data stored in your browser stays until you remove it (§3).

The site is served over HTTPS end to end. Since your family tree never leaves your device, the realistic risks to it are the ordinary ones — an unattended computer, a shared browser profile, or a saved file published somewhere by mistake. Keep backups of your GEDCOM files, and redact before you share: the application's Hide living people option and the Privacy tool exist for exactly that.

9Children

GED Merge is a research utility, not a service directed at children, and it collects nothing that could identify a visitor of any age. Family trees routinely contain data about children; handling that data responsibly is the user's own duty as controller (§6).

10Links to other sites

The application shows links that come out of your own file — archives, parish registers, cemetery records — and links of its own to gazetteers, map providers and this project's source code. Following one takes you to a site we do not operate and whose privacy practices are its own.

11Changes to this policy

GED Merge gains features regularly, and this policy is revised when a change affects what leaves your device. The effective and last-updated dates at the top of the page always say which version you are reading, and material changes are noted in the changelog. Continuing to use the site after a change means the current version applies.
